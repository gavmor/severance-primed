CHAPTER 01Welcome, Refiner

“The work is mysterious and important.”— ATTRIBUTED TO KIER EAGAN

Congratulations, refiner. You have been selected — from among many qualified candidates, and from among the hours of your own life — to serve Lumon Industries on the severed floor. This handbook contains everything your innie needs to know. Your outie has already read the other one.

You are one of Lumon's severed workers: people who agreed to have their memories surgically divided, so that the self who clocks in never knows the self who clocks out. By day you refine mysterious numbers in a windowless room. By night — in brief, stolen interludes — you glimpse the outie whose life paid for yours. Lumon calls the arrangement a gift. The Nine Principles say what you must become to deserve it.

none of this is true. all of it is true. — P.

What This Handbook Is

Genre, reverse-engineered: corporate dystopia × paranoid espionage thriller × religious cult drama. On the fantastic–realistic axis the game sits almost entirely on the realistic side: one science-fictional device (the severance chip) in an otherwise present-day world of fluorescent offices, company towns, and middle management. The horror is bureaucratic, not cosmic.

Theme: who owns a self — the person who suffers, or the company that bought the suffering? Every procedure in this handbook asks the same question two ways: what will you surrender to belong, and what will you risk to remember?

Mood: sterile, uncanny, retro-futurist dread — long fluorescent hallways, cheerful posters, a waffle party that feels like a threat — punctured by small, fierce human warmth: a shared joke in MDR, a map drawn from memory, a book passed hand to hand. Play it straight. Let the comedy come from the doctrine, never from the people.

The protagonists are severed workers — refiners, designers, goat-tenders — with no power except each other and the truth. The antagonist is not a person but a system: Lumon Industries, its doctrine, its cameras, its kindness. Named enforcers (Cobel, Milchick) appear as GMCs, but they are the doctrine's hands, not its heart. Per the Cortex heuristics, a system-antagonist means the game's opposition runs on the Doom Pool and Crisis Pools, not on contests against a faction.

CHAPTER 02The Nine Principles

“Nine principles. Nine tails. One path.”— OFFICE OF CULTURE & COMPLIANCE

Kier Eagan gave us nine principles, and the nine principles gave us everything. Study them. Internalize them. They are not rules; they are the shape of a good person. Below, each principle is shown with the virtue it claims, the work it quietly does, and the model statement your supervisor will expect to hear.

Three prime sets, plus Distinctions as always. There are no Attributes, no Skills, no Powers on the severed floor — Lumon hired your hands, not your résumé, and the chip took the rest.

Distinctions

Three per refiner, each rated d8 (roll d4 instead to gain 1 PP, per the standard rule). Severance imposes one structure on them: one distinction must belong to your outie (the life outside: My Outie Buried His Wife on a Tuesday), one must belong to your innie (the life at work: The Friendliest Refiner on the Floor), and one is free — the self in question, the part of you neither Lumon nor your outie can fully name (I Laugh When I Shouldn't).

The Nine Principles (Values)

The game's Values set is Kier Eagan's Nine Principles — the doctrine's virtues, each carrying its true function underneath. Every principle is rated d4–d12 and carries a one-line statement, written in Kier's liturgical voice or your own. The ratings measure how deeply the innie has internalized the doctrine — not how good they are.

PRINCIPLES IN PLAY

Uphold. When you act at real cost to honor a principle's statement — smiling through the grief, obeying the order that sickens you — step that principle up one die for the rest of the scene, and describe the doctrine speaking through you.

Challenge. When you violate a principle to act like a free person — asking the forbidden question, refusing the cheerful mask, turning your wiles against Lumon itself — gain 1 PP, and the GM steps up the lowest die in the Doom Pool. Lumon notices. The waffle party's nine-tailed whip has one tail for each principle; every challenge is a lash the doctrine remembers.

Relationships

Two sets, one awake at a time — the severance runs through your people too. This is the Mutually Exclusive limit: only the active set's dice can enter a pool. The other set is shut down.

  • Inside — awake on the severed floor. d10: one fellow innie you trust with your real thoughts. d8: one more inside figure — a coworker, or Management (Cobel, Milchick, Ms. Casey). Everyone else in here is d6.
  • Outside — shut down while you're innie. d8: one key person from your outie's life. Everyone else out there is d6. This set comes online during Outie Interludes — and only then, unless something breaks the rules.
  • d8 — My Outie: your relationship with your own other self. The one relationship severance can't cut — it is never shut down, whichever set is active. Roll it when you act on knowledge only your outie could have, when you defy your outie's wishes, or when the two of you — impossibly — seem to be working together.
MEMORANDUM — THE FLIP

Every severance transition flips the active set automatically: elevator down, Inside comes online and Outside shuts down; elevator up, the reverse. The flip itself pays nothing — it's just the premise. The Limit pays 1 PP when you shut down a set, once, at the moment of shutdown: switching sets mid-shift (the Overtime Contingency below), or sitting out a scene without your people (the set restores at the next scene).

Two things break the exclusion. The Overtime Contingency flips the set for a scene (below). A Bridge — a shared memory bought with growth — ignores it entirely; both selves can roll it. The third Bridge frays the severance for good, and the exclusion starts failing everywhere. That's the endgame.

Personal Effects (Signature Assets)

One per refiner, duly registered (or not): a single object the innie has hidden, found, or been given — Irving's hand-drawn map of the severed floor, a contraband copy of The You You Are, a note in my own handwriting I don't remember writing. Rated d8; it can be taken away, which is why it matters.

CHAPTER 03Intake & Calibration

“Bring your whole self to work. We will keep half.”— INTAKE POSTER, SEVERED LOBBY

Every refiner begins with calibration. Complete each step in order, in ink. Work through them together, out loud — the table should know every outie's reason, the way coworkers slowly learn each other's. If you do not know an answer, your supervisor does.

STEP 1 · DEPARTMENT ASSIGNMENT
☐ MACRODATA REFINEMENT — sorting numbers that feel wrong
☐ OPTICS & DESIGN — printing the doctrine's posters; nursing old rivalries
☐ MAMMALIANS NURTURABLE — tending the goats; no one will tell you why
☐ CHOREOGRAPHY & MERRIMENT

Departments are flavor and access, not dice — but where you work decides what you can reach.

STEP 2 · NAME (INNIE) & OUTIE'S REASON FOR SEVERANCE

First name + last initial, Lumon style. Then your outie's reason for severance, in one sentence — grief, debt, a crime, coercion, belief. The table hears it.

STEP 3 · DISTINCTIONS (THREE, EACH d8)

One outie, one innie, one free — the self in question, the part of you neither Lumon nor your outie can fully name.

STEP 4 · THE NINE PRINCIPLES

All begin at d8; step two up to d10 and two down to d6. Write a one-line statement for each. Your two d10s are the doctrine's hooks in you; your two d6s are where you're already slipping.

STEP 5 · RELATIONSHIPS (INSIDE / OUTSIDE · SEE CH. 2)

Rate per the spread in Chapter 2. Remember: only one set is awake at a time.

STEP 6 · PERSONAL EFFECT (d8)

Choose one object and say where it's hidden. If it is contraband, do not write that here.

STEP 7 · SECRET — FOR MANAGEMENT EYES ONLY

The one thing you are hiding from Lumon. (I found a message from my outie. I think Ms. Casey is someone's wife. I know what the goats are for.) This is campaign fuel; tell the GM, not necessarily the table. Fold along the dotted line.

Upon completion you will be issued 1 PP. The Four Tempers (Chapter 4) all begin unstressed. Welcome to Lumon.

CHAPTER 04Conduct & Correction

“Lumon corrects because Lumon cares.”— OFFICE OF CULTURE & COMPLIANCE

The procedures in this chapter are therapeutic instruments, calibrated over decades. Cooperate with them and they will never need to be used twice.

Tests, Contests, and Proper Channels

Default turn order is Dramatic — this is a game of glances, whispers, and break-room summons. Switch to Action Order only when the floor goes loud: a lockdown, a chase through the stairwell, the contingency going off.

Contests happen only against named GMCs — out-staring Milchick, lying to Cobel's face. You never roll against Lumon itself, the Board, or a department: those are systems, and systems are the Doom Pool and Crisis Pools.

The Nine (Doom Pool)

Lumon's doctrine, weaponized. The Doom Pool begins at d6 d6 and follows the standard rules — the GM adds a die on player opportunities (rolled 1s) — plus Severance-specific triggers:

  • Grows when a refiner challenges a Principle (step up the lowest die), when a shift ends with the File unrefined, when the Overtime Contingency is used, and after every Compunction.
  • Spends to interrupt a scene with Management, to inflict temper stress directly (Cobel's smile doesn't reach her eyes — take Dread stress), to summon a refiner to the Break Room or Wellness, to separate the party, or to reveal that a trusted routine was surveillance all along.
  • Escalation: once the pool holds d10s, the Board takes notice — new directives, new cameras, a visit. At d12 d12, the endgame unlocks: the testing floor doors open, or the Board convenes, and the season turns.
nine tails. one for each principle. count them at the waffle party. — P.

The Files (Crisis Pools) — Refinement

Each MDR file — Cold Harbor, Allentown, Siena — is a Crisis Pool (e.g. Cold Harbor: three d8s). A shift is a Timed Test: each refiner acts once, assembling a pool of a Principle (Wit, Nimbleness, and — daringly — Wiles) plus Distinctions and Relationships for teamwork. Effect dice step the File down; when the pool is gone, the file is complete — and something is revealed: a name, a face, a set of numbers that sorts toward someone you know.

Rolling a 1 while refining means the numbers got to you: take temper stress (GM's choice of Woe or Dread). This is the job's real wage.

The Four Tempers (Stress)

Kier taught that every worker carries four tempers to be tamed — Woe (melancholy, grief, inward despair), Frolic (levity, aimless indulgence), Dread (paralysis, apprehension), Malice (spite, defiance). Lumon calls them weaknesses. This handbook notes, for the record, that they are also the proof you're still human.

Each track steps d6 → d8 → d10 → d12. At d10 or higher the temper visibly warps your behavior — roleplay it; everyone at the table can see the crack.

COMPUNCTION

When a temper would step past d12, the refiner is summoned to the Break Room. Play the scene: the small room, the statement on the monitor, read aloud, read again, read until it sounds true. When it's over, the temper resets to d6 — but the GM adds a d8 to the Doom Pool and steps down one of the refiner's Principles. The doctrine corrects. It always collects its fee.

The Break Room, Wellness, and the Carrot

The Break Room is the stick (above). Wellness sessions with Ms. Casey are the carrot: a refiner who completes a wellness session steps down one temper of their choice — but Ms. Casey always asks one question she shouldn't be able to ask, about the outie's life. Answering truthfully creates an Asset for Management (the GM notes it); deflecting is a Contest. Nothing at Lumon is free.

Waffle parties and Music Dance Experiences work the same way at group scale: tempers step down across the department, and the Doom Pool grows a die. Joy, metered and priced.

The Overtime Contingency

Spend 1 PP and add a d8 to the Doom Pool: flip the active relationship set for one scene — your outie wakes inside. Inside shuts down (the Limit: gain 1 PP, so the contingency costs you a Doom die, not plot points); Outside comes online. Roll only outie-side traits — Outie distinction, free distinction, Outside relationships (and My Outie) — no Principles; the doctrine doesn't recognize this person. What the outie does with the body, the innie will never remember — unless someone was watching.

it works. that’s the worst part. — P.

Resignation Requests

Filing one is a Test against the Doom Pool. Success means the request reaches your outie. Whether the outie honors it — whether they even open it — is a scene for an Outie Interlude, and the GM is under no obligation to be kind.

CHAPTER 05Beyond the Building

“Your outie maintains a life beyond these walls so that you don’t have to.”— ORIENTATION DAY REMARKS

What follows is provided for context only. Do not romanticize it.

Outie Interludes

Between shifts, each player frames a short scene as their outie — the parking lot, the apartment, the support group for the severed. Outies assemble pools from Distinctions and Relationships only: no Principles. The doctrine doesn't live outside the building. The Inside set is shut down out here — only Outside relationships (and My Outie) are on the table.

Discoveries made out here become Assets the innie finds without knowing why: a note in your own handwriting — don't trust the numbers ( d8 ). The GM may also frame an interlude against the player: the outie considering quitting, the outie meeting someone reintegrated, the outie doing something the innie would hate them for. The two of you share a body and nothing else — act like it.

Reintegration (Growth Pool)

Use the Growth Pool. At session's end, nominate moments when the selves bled through — a memory that surfaced mid-shift, an outie dreaming the fluorescent halls. Spend growth to buy Bridges: a shared memory both selves can roll, written as a trait both halves of the character sheet can reach (The Lake House, Age Nine — d8). Bridges are usable by innie and outie, Principles or no Principles. A Bridge ignores the Mutually Exclusive limit. The third Bridge frays the severance for good — that character's endgame has begun, and Lumon will do anything to stop it.

FINAL SECTIONOrientation Day

“Your first day follows the schedule below. Deviations will be noted.”— OFFICE OF CULTURE & COMPLIANCE

Situation. A new file, COLD HARBOR, has landed in Macrodata Refinement with a quota and a deadline. Management announces a "wellness initiative": mandatory sessions for all refiners. And Petra D., the department's senior refiner, hasn't come back from the Break Room — it's been three days, and her desk is exactly as she left it. The refiners are the ones left holding the file.

Reveals (one per refiner, plus the floor):

  • For the new hire: the orientation video addresses you by a name that isn't yours — then corrects itself. No one else reacts.
  • For the veteran: the numbers in Cold Harbor sort toward a face you almost recognize. Roll Woe or look away.
  • For the believer: Ms. Casey asks about your outie's childhood home. You never told her your outie had one.
  • For the floor: the Break Room door is warm to the touch, like something in there has been breathing.

Opening scenes:

  1. The video. Kier's voice over the Perpetuity Wing tour: the Nine Principles recited like scripture. Each player states which principle their innie clings to — and which one already itches.
  2. First shift. Cold Harbor on the screens (Crisis Pool, three d8s). Refine. The numbers feel wrong for exactly one refiner — their choice which, and why.
  3. The summons. Split the party: one refiner to Wellness (Ms. Casey, one impossible question), one to the Break Room (Petra D.'s chair is still warm). The rest watch the hallway and learn what surveillance feels like.
